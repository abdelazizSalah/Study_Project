Task1 a.) Runtime and RAM measurement:

Autoencoder stage: The autoencoder training is consistently expensive and takes minutes per run (RAW: 531.73 s, RE15: 436.49 s).
Feature extraction is much faster than training (RAW: 36.97 s, RE15: 29.72 s), but it is the most memory-demanding stage in the whole pipeline, reaching the highest peak RAM values (RAW: 39.05 GB, RE15: 35.42 GB).
Autoencoder training itself also shows a clear memory difference between feature types: peak RAM during training is higher for RE15 (28.51 GB) than for RAW (15.59 GB), while extraction is very memory-intensive for both.

Classifier RAM: Peak RAM usage is generally very similar between training and testing for a given classifier (differences are small compared to the overall level).
For RAW features, memory consumption depends strongly on the scenario: scenario 1 has comparatively low peak RAM (roughly 3–4 GB across the tested classifiers), whereas scenarios 2 and 3 show clearly higher peak RAM values (roughly 5–9 GB),
with the overall maximum occurring for bSVM in scenario 2 (about 9.1 GB). For RE15 features, peak RAM is significantly lower in scenarios 1 and 2 (around 1.2–1.5 GB),
but scenario 3 stands out with substantially higher peak RAM (around 4.8 GB for the measured classifiers). Overall, RAW shows “low RAM in scenario 1, high RAM in scenarios 2 and 3”,
while RE15 shows “low RAM in scenarios 1 and 2, but a clear increase in scenario 3”.

Classifier runtime: For most classifiers, training takes longer than testing; however, there are important exceptions.
kNN has an almost zero training time but a much larger testing time because prediction requires distance computations to many stored training points, which is especially visible for RAW features.
In addition, LOF in the RAW setting (scenario 1) shows a longer testing time than training (train: 50.47 s vs. test: 104.18 s). For RAW features, runtimes vary by several orders of magnitude: the longest training time occurs for bSVM
in scenario 2 (25668.30 s), and bSVM also has very large testing time there (6014.90 s). EE and OCSVM also have very high training costs in scenario 1 (EE: 3010.66 s, OCSVM: 1730.00 s), while kNN has by far the smallest training time (≈0.20 s).
The smallest testing times are achieved by Random Forest (sub-second testing in scenarios 2 and 3: 0.44 s and 0.36 s). The largest train–test gaps in RAW occur for bSVM, EE, RF, and kNN (either due to expensive training with cheap testing,
or vice versa for kNN). For RE15 features, classifier runtimes drop dramatically and become very small overall: EE training remains the slowest (43.52 s), but all other training times are in the sub-second to ~1 second range,
and testing times are consistently sub-second and relatively similar across classifiers (roughly 0.01–0.27 s). This indicates that with RE15 the classifier stage is no longer the bottleneck,
while with RAW the classifier choice can dominate runtime (especially for bSVM and kNN).
